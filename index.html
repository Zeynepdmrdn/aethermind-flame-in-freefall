How the retrieval works
  1. Each passage is a short quotation from a NASA source, tagged with study, evidence type (reported finding, test condition or study objective), source URL and PDF slide number where one applies. Passages also carry verified metadata: platform, vehicle, sample material or fuel, and the variables measured or varied.
  2. Your question and the passages are lower-cased, split into words, stripped of common words and lightly stemmed (for example detected, detection and detector all reduce to detect).
  3. A small hand-written synonym map links domain terms (for example airflow, ventilation and flow). A synonym match counts 0.6 of an exact match.
  4. Passages are scored with BM25F (BM25 with weighted fields: text 2, metadata 1.5, label 0.5; k1 1.5, b 0.75). Rare words count for more than common ones.
  5. If you name a study (Saffire, FLEX, SoFIE or MIST), only that study's passages are searched; the name itself is not counted as a match.
  6. A passage is shown only if it covers at least 40% of your question's weighted terms, at least 75% of the best passage's coverage, and at least 60% of the best score. If none qualifies, the result is "Insufficient evidence".
  7. If you ask for a value (a concentration, a pressure, how long, when) and no returned passage gives a number with a unit, the passages are labelled "Related, not an answer".

Search is keyword matching, not AI. It can miss a passage that uses different words and can return loosely related passages for short questions. Read the source and its scope before relying on a passage.

How the ordering works: fixed topic rules, not AI. Each study gets points for the selected topic: 2 = its reported finding or stated objective directly addresses the topic; 1 = mentioned only as context; 0 = not mentioned. Higher points come first; ties keep the original order (Saffire VI, FLEX, SoFIE-MIST). The points are a sorting aid only, not a measure of importance, safety or risk.
